## Concede the settlement records, then ask about everything else

ARC's core function is financial settlement between travel agencies and carriers,
and some of what it holds is transactional record-keeping subject to retention
obligations. The letter says so in its own words and offers that as a complete
answer where it applies — because a request that ignores the obvious exemption
invites a reply scoped entirely to it.

What it asks for beyond that:

- **The travel records themselves** — itineraries, ticket numbers, routings,
  dates, issuing agencies, forms of payment, loyalty identifiers. The subject
  never had a relationship with ARC; the records exist because other parties
  transacted about them.
- **Derived data products and their licensees.** ARC licenses travel data
  commercially, and a deletion that leaves copies with a licensee is a deletion
  in name only.
- **Government disclosure, as three specific questions** rather than a general
  one: has any record been supplied to a government agency; if so which agencies,
  when, and under what instrument (subpoena, court order, or commercial
  agreement); and does ARC notify individuals when it happens. This is framed
  around what has been publicly *reported* about a traveller-data programme,
  not asserted as established fact — and each question has an acceptable answer,
  including "a legal prohibition prevents notification, and here is the
  instrument."
- **Do-not-license as a standing entry**, so future ticketing is not fed into
  commercial products.

**The processor deflection is pre-empted:** if ARC says it acts for carriers and
agencies rather than its own account, the letter accepts that and asks it to name
the controllers — because that answer without names leaves a right with nobody to
exercise it against.
