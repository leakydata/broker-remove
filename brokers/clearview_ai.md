Core distinction pressed: THE FACEPRINT IS THE THING, NOT THE PHOTOGRAPH. A scraped image is a copy of something already visible; the biometric vector computed from it is generated by Clearview, exists nowhere else, and is what turns an unremarkable photo into a searchable index entry tying a face to a name, employer and address. Asked them to confirm the TEMPLATES are deleted, not just the images. Also asked: (1) durable do-not-re-enrol suppression, since their DB is built by continuous crawling and a deletion without it is undone by the next crawl of the same page - and if suppression requires keeping a vector, which list it sits in; (2) SEARCH HISTORY - whether any client query has ever returned a match to me, how many times, over what period, noting their clients include law enforcement and a person who has appeared in such a search has an obvious interest in knowing; (3) source URLs so I can approach those sites. Pre-empted 'publicly available': publication makes an image viewable by people who visit that page, it does not make the subject searchable by face across the internet by anyone holding a photo - that capability was created by Clearview and is what the request is about. On verification, refused account creation and ID upload with a reason specific to this company: requiring a person to hand a facial recognition company a NEW high-quality photo of their face plus a legal-name document, in order to be removed from a facial recognition database, demands materially more identifying data than the removal concerns. Asked for a clear refusal I can record if that is their process.
