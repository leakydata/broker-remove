## Gotchas

**This company was mistakenly treated as an alias of a different, unrelated
broker for two sends before anyone actually wrote to it correctly.** A prior
pass, working the `data_direct_marketing` (datadirectmarketing.com) entry after
its own address bounced, guessed that `teamdms.com` was a "corrected address"
for the *same* company and resent the Data-Direct-Marketing letter there —
addressed to "the Privacy Officer at Data Direct Marketing," not to Direct
Marketing Solutions, Inc. On top of that, the address was mistyped as
`datanalytics@teamdms.com` (missing the second "a"), which hard-bounced. The
bounce was correctly diagnosed and the bad guess was cleared from
`data_direct_marketing`'s registry entry — but nobody circled back to check
whether `teamdms.com` was actually a distinct, legitimate broker in its own
right with a curated registry entry already sitting unused
(`direct_marketing_solutions`, sourced from the CA data broker registry, with
the correctly-spelled `dataanalytics@teamdms.com`).

**The lesson:** a same-looking company name plus a plausible domain match is not
evidence of the same company. `datadirectmarketing.com` and `teamdms.com` are
two different domains, two different registry entries, and — as far as this
project can tell — two different companies that happen to both do list/data
services with "direct marketing" in the pitch. Verify a corrected address
against the *registry entry for that specific domain* before reusing it for a
different one, and check whether the "new" domain already has its own untouched
entry before assuming it's a fallback for something else.
