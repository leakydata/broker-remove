## Steps

1. **Email `info@dlwichita.com`.** The registry domain is `downingandlahey.com`
   (Optery's listing), but that site itself publishes no contact address; the
   working address found is on `dlwichita.com` — almost certainly the same
   funeral home's actual operating domain (Downing & Lahey is based in
   Wichita, KS), not an unrelated third party.
2. **Expect this to be an odd one.** The broker is a funeral home / mortuary
   whose site apparently republishes obituary and death-record content in a
   form Optery classifies as "People Search." A deletion request here is
   really asking a small local business to take down or suppress a published
   obituary page, which is a different kind of removal than a compiled-profile
   broker — be prepared for a reply that doesn't know what a CCPA request is.
3. **Ask plainly**: is any page on either domain publishing my name, and if
   so, will they take it down or deindex it. A funeral home has no sale-of-data
   business to opt out of; the ask here is really "remove/deindex the page."

## Gotchas

- **This may not behave like a data broker at all.** If the reply (or no
  reply) suggests this is a small business with no privacy process, the
  statutory framing may be wasted on them — a plain "please remove this page"
  request might work better on a second attempt.
