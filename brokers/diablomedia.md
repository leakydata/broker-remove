2. Ask for the acquisition source — publisher, affiliate, list, and date.
3. Ask which clients or partners the data was sold or passed to.
4. Ask for **permanent suppression**, in those words, not deletion.

## Gotchas

Affiliate and performance marketing, which shapes what a good outcome is.

**Deletion alone is close to worthless in this business model.** The record was
acquired from an upstream publisher or list, and the same upstream source will be
ingested again. A broker who deletes without suppressing has honoured the request
exactly as worded and will hold the record again within a refresh cycle — and
you will have no way of knowing until you write to them a second time.

Always ask for suppression explicitly, and ask them to confirm which of the two
they applied. "Your data has been deleted" answers the question you asked, not the
one you meant.

The acquisition-source question is the other half. In this category the consumer
usually has no memory of ever providing the data, and the answer names an upstream
relationship — a publisher, a lead list, a co-registration partner — which is
itself a broker worth adding to the registry. See `_CATEGORY_VARIANTS.md` on the
lead-gen variant.

## Verification

No public listing to check. The written answer carries the whole weight, so ask
for it to be specific: which identifiers were found, what was applied
(suppression or deletion), where the data came from, and who it went to.

Re-check by writing again after a refresh cycle — roughly 60–90 days — if the
reply confirmed deletion but not suppression.
