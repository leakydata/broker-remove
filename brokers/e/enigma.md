## Steps

1. Email `privacy@enigma.com`.
2. Pre-empt the business-data deflection in the letter itself — see below.
3. Ask specifically whether personal identifiers appear as owner, officer or
   contact details on a business record.
4. Ask for **modelled attributes** — revenue, transaction volume, card activity,
   risk and stability scores — not only the identifying fields.
5. If they decline, ask for the refusal in writing.

## Gotchas

**"That's business data, not consumer data" is only half an answer**, and it is the
half a small-business data provider will reach for first.

For a sole proprietorship, a single-member LLC or a home-based business, the
business record *is* a personal record: the contact name is a person's name, the
business address is a home address, the business phone is a personal mobile. And
the modelled attributes attached to it — estimated revenue, transaction volume,
card activity, creditworthiness, stability scores — are inferences about one
identifiable individual, however they are labelled in the schema.

So do not argue the general point. Ask the specific questions the general point
implies:

- Do any of my personal identifiers appear in your business records as contact,
  owner, officer or principal details?
- Do you hold a record associating my name with a business, and what does it
  contain?
- What modelled attributes are attached to it?

Then ask for a **written refusal** if they still decline. A documented "we consider
this business data and will not action your request" is worth having; an
unanswered request is worth nothing, and the two are easy to confuse from outside.
