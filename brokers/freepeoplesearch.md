## Gotchas

Same shape as `fastpeoplesearch_io.md` and `findpeoplefast_net.md`, and the same
two asks that routinely go missing.

**Former identifiers**, because the index is built from them: a search of current
details finds one profile, that profile is removed, and everything filed under a
previous address or a disconnected number stays live.

**Relatives and associates**, because listings are reciprocal: a profile-scoped
deletion leaves the subject named on other people's pages as a "related person",
and that is frequently how someone is found in the first place. No confirmation
will mention it, because the request was read as being about a profile.

## Verification

Re-run the public search on **each** former address and each old number
separately, not just on the name. Then search a relative's listing for the
subject's name in the related-persons block — that is where a profile-scoped
removal leaves residue, and no confirmation will mention it.

## Confirmed, after a three-stage queue (updated 2026-08-19)

> "We've received your request to remove your record. We're glad to confirm that
> the entry for `<NAME>` has been successfully removed."

Recorded `confirmed`. The route ran in three stages over about 33 hours, and the
first two are easy to mistake for the last:

1. **Instant holding reply** — *"We are currently managing an increased volume of
   requests. We appreciate your patience as our team works through them."* This
   is an autoresponder. It is not an acknowledgement that anything was read.
2. **The locating-details demand**, several hours later — the same shape as the
   sibling site's: name, city/state, street address, profile link.
3. **The confirmation**, the next day.

> **A "high volume of requests" holding message resets no clock and proves
> nothing.** It is worth exactly one thing: evidence the address is live. Keep the
> entry at `submitted` and wait for a message that names a record.

### Answer the details demand without conceding the search

The reply to stage 2 declined to treat a profile URL as a precondition, and said
so plainly: the operator is better placed to search its own index by the
identifiers already supplied than the consumer is to hand over a link for each
record. That framing worked here — the confirmation arrived without a URL hunt
across every variant.

> **Supply what you have, but say out loud that a link is not a requirement.**
> Otherwise "please send the profile link" quietly converts an
> all-records request into a one-page request, and the confirmation you eventually
> get will be scoped to whatever you happened to find.
