## Gotchas

**The mailbox name is a scope claim, and it should be answered as one.** Their
only published privacy address is named for California consumers and the CCPA. A
consumer outside California reading that address has been told, without anyone
having to say it, that the channel is not for them — and most will not write.

So write, and say why:

> *"I am writing to it because it is the channel you have provided, and I would
> ask you not to treat the name of the mailbox as the scope of your obligations or
> of your policy. If your position is that you will only action requests from
> residents of particular states, I ask you to tell me that in writing."*

This costs a paragraph and converts a silent deterrent into either an actioned
request or a documented refusal. Both are better than not writing. See
`_DEFLECTIONS.md` §20 for the same move against a jurisdictional reply — the
principle is identical: never let a scope *claim* do the work of a scope
*decision*.

**Household identifiers are the substance here.** Television advertising targets
households, not people, so the record is likely keyed to an IP-derived dwelling
association rather than to a name. That makes it address-adjacent by design, and a
household record is personal information about the people in the household.

Ask separately whether deletion removes the **ad-exposure records** — which
advertisements were delivered to a device or household — or only the mapping. As
with location data, the mapping is the cheap half.
