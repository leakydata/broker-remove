## Gotchas

A large education company holds personal data in systems that have almost nothing
to do with each other, and a single privacy mailbox will answer for whichever one
the responder owns. Name them.

- **Learning-platform records** are usually held *for an institution*, so expect
  the processor answer. Ask them to name the institution and to pass the request
  on — a processor is normally expected to do that, and asking makes the refusal
  costly if they decline both.
- **Marketing and prospect data** is the part acquired rather than given, and the
  part the person has no knowledge of. Ask where it came from: an institution's
  list, a testing programme, a student-search service, a compiler.
- **Published and archival products** are genuinely different. If a name appears
  in a digitised collection or directory Gale licenses, that may be legitimate
  archival material rather than a marketing record. Ask to be told what and from
  where, rather than assuming the answer either way — a request that demands
  deletion of an archive without asking first invites a refusal that then colours
  the rest of the reply.

**The `.edu` address is the key that matters.** In the education sector it is
frequently the only identifier a record is held under, and it is easy to skim past
in a list of consumer addresses. Call it out by name.

## Verification

No public listing. Ask which of the three estates held a record, what the source
