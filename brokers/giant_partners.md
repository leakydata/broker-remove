## Gotchas

The usual suppression-versus-deletion point applies (`dmdatabases_com.md`), but a
multichannel marketing business adds a sharper version of it worth stating
separately.

**Deleting a phone number can un-suppress it.** If a number is on an internal
do-not-call or do-not-text list, that entry is often a property of the record.
Delete the record and the suppression can go with it — so the number becomes
eligible for calling again *precisely because* the consumer asked for their data
to be deleted. The request produced the opposite of its purpose, and nothing in
the confirmation would reveal it.

So ask for both, explicitly: delete the record, **and** place the numbers on the
suppression lists as standalone entries. Any competent compliance team will
recognise the problem immediately; the ones that do not are the ones where it
matters most.

The same logic applies to email addresses and unsubscribe lists, though the
consequences are milder.
