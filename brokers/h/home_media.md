Home-services lead gen matches consumers enquiring about home improvement with contractors, so a record usually ORIGINATES AS A FORM SUBMISSION and is then sold onward -- and two consequences follow that are invisible from outside. FIRST, A LEAD ALREADY SOLD IS BEYOND RECALL: deleting their copy does not retrieve the buyers' copies, so the letter asks whether a record about the subject has EVER BEEN SUPPLIED TO A BUYER, and roughly when and how many -- counts, dates and categories only, explicitly not asking any contractor to be named. SECOND, THE RECORD MAY NOT HAVE ORIGINATED WITH HIM: this data arrives through affiliates, co-registration paths and partner sites, and consumers routinely have no memory of a form they never knowingly filled in, so the letter asks WHAT SOURCE OR PARTNER supplied any record and on what date, noting that a record he did not create is the single most useful thing they could report. ALSO ASKED: whether they hold a CONSENT RECORD (TCPA consent, timestamp, IP, form URL) and what it SAYS rather than merely that it exists -- because if a consent was created in his name that he did not give, its contents are the point; what is inferred rather than collected (property value, homeownership, likely project type, income band); and whether the suppression is forward-looking against incoming affiliate feeds, named as the only one of the four asks that survives a month. Standard limit included: suppress the ASSOCIATION rather than the bare value for former addresses and reassigned numbers.
