- Note: Sent the LOCATION-DATA variant per _CATEGORY_VARIANTS.md rather than the generic letter. The core move is the GEOGRAPHIC QUERY WITH CONFIRM-BEFORE-DELETE: since the record is keyed to a mobile advertising identifier that the subject cannot supply -- platforms do not reliably show it, a resettable one has changed many times over the period a history covers, and any value readable today would not match historical rows -- the letter instead asks them to query for devices showing a PERSISTENT OVERNIGHT DWELL at the listed residential addresses over the periods lived at each, and to CONFIRM THE MATCH BACK BEFORE DELETING ANYTHING. That is safe to offer and hard to refuse: nothing is deleted until verified, so no other person's records can be touched by a loose match, and it preserves the whole protective purpose of verification while using only what the subject can actually give. PO boxes were deliberately omitted and the omission explained -- a device does not dwell at a post office box overnight. Closed with 'if this method does not fit your systems, tell me what would -- I am asking you to name a route that exists, not to waive the check.'

Also asked by name for: the advertising/device identifiers; location and movement history rather than the derived segments; INFERRED HOME AND WORK, which is the join between an anonymous device and a named person; identity-graph linkage; and forward-looking suppression, since the next feed from the same SDK restores everything. Two pre-empts: name the identifier types searched, because otherwise a nil return may only mean a never-populated name field was searched; and aggregation does not help, because traces derived from individual devices are personal information even where the published output is aggregate. Asked about SENSITIVE-LOCATION inferences by name -- worship, medical, legal, shelters, union halls, protest sites -- including where they conclude they may lawfully retain them. Cited 1798.121 on precise geolocation as sensitive personal information.
