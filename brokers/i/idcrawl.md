## Gotchas

**The aggregation is the harm, and saying so avoids a dead-end argument.** Every
profile such a site surfaces may be public on its own platform, and a request that
looks like "remove this public thing" invites the obvious reply. What the
aggregator produced that no source did is the **composite**: social accounts,
usernames, photographs, employer, location and public records, side by side, keyed
to one name. That composite is theirs, and it is what to ask for.

**Ask for the handle associations by name.** If the index links a person's real
name to social media usernames, that link can survive the removal of a profile
page and keep surfacing. It is also frequently the single most identifying thing
the site holds — a pseudonymous account is only pseudonymous until something joins
it to a legal name.

**Ask for crawl exclusion, not just deletion.** A site rebuilt from the open web
re-creates the page at the next pass unless the name is excluded. This is the
suppression-versus-deletion distinction in its crawler form, and it is the version
where deletion alone is most obviously temporary.

## Verification

Re-run the site's search on the name, and separately on each known username or
handle — the second is where residue survives a page removal. Re-check after a
crawl cycle if the reply confirmed deletion but not exclusion.
