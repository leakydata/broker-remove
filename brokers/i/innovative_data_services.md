(2) THEY HONOURED THE 193 LIMIT AND STATED IT BETTER THAN I PUT IT: 'Our suppression process applies to records associated with the individual making the request; IT DOES NOT SUPPRESS A POSTAL ADDRESS ITSELF or records associated with other individuals or household members who have not separately requested suppression. Accordingly, the prior addresses you provided have not been suppressed as addresses.' That is the ask companies most often get wrong in my favour -- a bare-address exclusion in a direct-mail file stops mail to a household where strangers now live. (3) ONGOING SUPPRESSION, NOT A ONE-TIME REMOVAL: 'We also maintain information necessary to recognize and honor consumer privacy requests on an ongoing basis' -- which is what makes the deletion survive the next file they acquire, and in a list business acquiring files IS the business. DECLINED AS A CATEGORY: 'We do not provide information regarding internal data architecture, migration processes, source files, matching methodologies, or records concerning other consumers.' That covers the acquisition question -- whether the predecessor's suppression list survived the 15 June transfer. Recorded as unanswered rather than refused, not pressed, and I explained once why it is worth asking: if a suppression list does not survive an acquisition NOTHING SHOWS -- no records are missing, there are simply more of them, match rates and coverage improve, and the only people who could detect the loss are those who asked to be excluded and are by definition not looking.
