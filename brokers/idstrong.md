## Gotchas

Two businesses under one name, and a request that does not distinguish them gets
answered by whichever the responder owns: an identity-monitoring service, and a
publicly-searchable people-search index. Say which you mean and ask them to say
which they searched.

**The breach-data ask is the distinctive one.** Services like this compile
breached datasets in order to alert people that their information is exposed. The
intention is protective and the effect is that a commercial company you have no
relationship with holds a consolidated copy of your breached data. Ask for it to
be deleted along with everything else, and say you understand the intent — the
request is more likely to be engaged with than dismissed if it does not read as an
accusation.

**Refuse the identity document in advance, and give the reason.** Sending a
passport or driving licence to a company whose business is compiling exposed
personal data is a poor trade, and stating that plainly makes the refusal a
position rather than an obstruction. Similarly, refuse in advance to sign up for a
monitoring product: the request is to stop holding the information, not to enter
into a relationship.

## Their ticket number gave away a family

Request `538837`. That number matters more than the request it identifies:

    537420   InfoTracer      (and its 46 state arrest-record sites)
    538631   CourtCaseFinder
    538837   IDStrong

Three brands, one helpdesk sequence. The numbers are assigned by the vendor rather
than chosen by the company, so a shared sequence normally means a shared tenant —
see `_BROKER_FAMILIES.md`, which now carries the detection and its limits.

**Asked rather than assumed.** A shared Zendesk instance is not a shared database:
two firms can use one vendor, or an agency can run several clients' desks. So the
follow-up puts three possible answers to them and says each is useful — same
operation (then one request covers all, and name the properties), same vendor only
(then pursue separately, nothing lost), or some other relationship (then name the
entity that actually holds the records).

That framing matters. A question invites an answer; an accusation invites a denial,
and a denial to an assertion you cannot prove leaves you worse off than before you
asked.
