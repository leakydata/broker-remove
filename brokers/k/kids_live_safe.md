## A confirmation that names the record back

> *"This email is to confirm that we have removed the public record information for
> &lt;NAME&gt; of &lt;CITY&gt;, PA, age &lt;AGE&gt; from the search results of
> KidsLiveSafe.com."*

Two things make this a strong artifact rather than a polite noise.

**It names the record.** Name, city and age, read back. That is implicit
confirmation that a record **was** held -- compare the far more common *"if present,
you will be removed"*, which leaves the requester unable to tell a completed removal
from an empty database. Nothing here is conditional.

**It states its own scope**: "from the search results of KidsLiveSafe.com". That is
honest, and it is also the limit. It says nothing about data licensed onward, and
nothing about other properties. Worth reading as what it is rather than as more than
it is.

## They flag the cache lag, which most do not

> *"even though the data has been removed from our website it may take several days
> to several weeks for search engines like Google and Bing to update these changes
> in their search results."*

This matters for verification. Re-checking too early and finding the listing in a
search-engine result would look like a failed removal or a reappearance, when it is
a cached copy of a page that no longer exists. **Verify against the broker's own
site, not against a search engine**, and give cached results weeks rather than days
before treating them as evidence of anything.
