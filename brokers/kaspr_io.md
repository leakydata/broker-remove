## The best disclosure in the project so far

Kaspr answered a deletion request by naming exactly what they held **and where
each field came from**. Almost nobody does the second part.

| Field | Source they named |
|---|---|
| Name | — |
| Company and job title | — |
| Business email address | **"Kaspr Research"** |
| Two telephone numbers | **People Data Labs** |
| LinkedIn profile URL | **CoreSignal** |

And they explained what the first one means, unprompted:

> *"Kaspr Research refers to the fact that the email was generated by our own
> internal tool based on your work experience."*

**That single sentence is the most useful thing any broker has said in this
project.** It confirms directly what `_DEFLECTIONS.md` §44 argued from the
outside: a B2B contact database's work email addresses are frequently
*manufactured* from name-and-employer patterns rather than observed. The
consumer never chose it, has never seen it, and cannot supply it — which is why
"what other email formats do you use?" is an unanswerable question, and why a
null result on personal webmail means nothing.

Now it does not have to be argued. It can be quoted.

## What they did

> *"your personal data has been deleted from our active systems. Your details
> have also been added to our suppression list for the sole purpose of preventing
> your information from being reintroduced into our database in the future...
> the suppression record contains only the minimum information necessary."*

Deletion **plus** scoped suppression, plus a recorded opt-out from sale and
sharing, plus an objection to further processing for direct marketing and B2B
prospecting. They also stated plainly that no recipients had received the data,
so there was nothing to propagate.

That is the standard to hold others to, and the letter to Cloudlead already
quotes it.

## The part that needs the subject, not the agent

Two of the disclosed fields are identifiers this project has never held:

- a **business email at an employer domain**, which Kaspr says their own tool
  *generated* rather than observed — so it may not exist at all;
- **two telephone numbers** from People Data Labs, same area code as the
  subject's but matching none of the numbers on file.

**Queued as a decision rather than assumed.** If the numbers are the subject's,
adding them to the profile is a real coverage gain for every future letter. If
they are not, they belong to a stranger and are attached to the subject's name in
a database that sells them — which is worth telling People Data Labs directly,
and is a different request entirely.

Asserting a number is yours when it is not would breach the rule against guessing
which record belongs to whom, and would put someone else's phone number into a
letter. Not a call to make on the subject's behalf.
