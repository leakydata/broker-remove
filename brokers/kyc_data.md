## Gotchas

**Deletion is not automatically the right ask at an identity-verification company.**

At a marketing broker the record is a nuisance. Here it can decide whether a person
can open an account, pass a check, or be believed about who they are. That inverts
the usual order:

- **Ask what exists first.** Which identifiers, which categories, which sources. A
  disclosure is worth more here than a deletion, because you cannot tell whether
  the record helps or harms you until you have seen it.
- **Prefer correction where the record is wrong.** An inaccurate identity record
  deleted from one file persists at whoever supplied it and returns at the next
  ingest; a corrected one is fixed at source. Deleting a wrong record can also
  destroy the evidence that it was wrong, which matters if it has already caused a
  decision.

**On legal-retention claims, split the two things.** These businesses often must
retain verification records. That is a reason to keep something; it is not a reason
to keep **selling** it. Ask them to confirm that anything retained under an
obligation is used *only* to meet it — not for sale, sharing, marketing or model
training. A company that agrees has given you most of what a deletion would.

## Verification

Nothing public to search. The disclosure is the artifact: ask for what they hold,

record turns out to be inaccurate, keep the correspondence — it is the evidence if
the error has already affected something.
