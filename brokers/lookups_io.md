## The one case where "send us the URL" is legitimate

`_DEFLECTIONS.md` §28 is about refusing to do a company's searching for it. This is
the exception the same section tells you to look for, and it is worth recognising so
the principle does not harden into a reflex.

Lookups.io's removal tool takes a **profile URL** and nothing else:

> *"Remove a profile from Lookups.io search results. We'll verify your email before
> making any changes."*

That is a real constraint on the tool, not a way of shifting work. The offer made to
LicenseFiles and Thryv — *if your tool genuinely takes a URL as input, say so and
point me at it* — was made in good faith, and here it is met. So search their site.

## Their search returns several people with the same name

A name-and-city search returned records across four sources, and the ages do not
agree with each other or with the subject:

  - two profiles in the right city and the right ZIP, carrying ages **43** and **42**,
    one showing a masked `+1 (717)` number matching the area code of four prior
    numbers;
  - a same-name profile in the right city aged **80**;
  - a **different first name** on the same surname, aged 80, in the same city;
  - several out-of-state entries.

Brokers routinely carry a wrong age, so an age mismatch is weak evidence of anything
— which cuts both ways. It does not rule the 42 and 43 profiles out, and it does not
rule the 80 one in.

**Do not guess.** This tool removes whatever profile it is given, and an incorrect
submission suppresses a stranger's listing. That is a real harm to a third party who
never asked for it, and it is not recoverable by apologising afterwards. Collect the
candidate URLs, put the identification to the person whose data it is, and submit only
what they confirm.

The masked details are the only discriminator available without paying, and paying is
not on the table — the full profile sits behind an "Unlock Profile" paywall, and this
project does not create accounts or buy access to a person's own data.
