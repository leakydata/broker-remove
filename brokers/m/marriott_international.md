- Note: Emailed privacy@marriott.com 2026-08-28 (CA registry 2025-2026). FIRST-PARTY BUSINESS ON THE BROKER REGISTRY -- a category the project has not handled before, and the letter is shaped around the risk that a blanket deletion costs the subject something. It opens by saying plainly: NOT asking to close or cancel a Bonvoy account, forfeit points, or erase a booking or billing record, and if the request would do any of those, STOP AND ASK FIRST. Then splits the ask: unconditional opt-out and marketing suppression, which touch no account and destroy nothing; and deletion only of what is NOT required for an active account, completed transaction, legal obligation or fraud purposes -- specifically marketing profiles, inferred preferences, segments, propensity scores, and anything ACQUIRED FROM OR SHARED WITH third parties rather than generated by his own dealings with them. THE QUESTION THAT PROMPTED THE LETTER: a data broker is by definition a business that sells personal information about consumers it has NO DIRECT RELATIONSHIP with, so what is the data that made Marriott a registrant? Which activity involves people who are not its guests? A one-line answer changes what to ask for. Also named stay history as sensitive in a way worth stating -- where a person was, on what dates, sometimes with whom, which can reveal a medical treatment, a religious observance, a legal proceeding or a relationship -- and asked for use-and-disclosure limitation separately from deletion. No card number, passport number or SSN sent, and all three refused in advance.
