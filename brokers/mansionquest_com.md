So every row is A NAMED INDIVIDUAL, THEIR EXACT STREET ADDRESS AND A DESCRIPTION OF THEIR HOME, in a sortable filterable 1,000-row DataTable. Built from Florida county property appraiser rolls, which I conceded openly. THE HONEST POSITION, STATED RATHER THAN ARGUED AROUND: California excludes PUBLICLY AVAILABLE information -- incl. lawfully-available government records -- from personal information, and on its face that covers them. I said so instead of pretending otherwise. But 1798.140(v)(2) attaches a condition that is easy to miss: information is NOT publicly available if used for a purpose INCOMPATIBLE with the purpose for which it is maintained and made available in the government records. Appraiser rolls are maintained for ad valorem TAX ASSESSMENT; a ranked wealth list published as a sponsored media product is a different purpose. Put as a QUESTION -- have you considered it, what view did you take -- not as a conclusion, and said explicitly that 'we rely on the exclusion and think the condition is satisfied' is a good answer I would record as theirs. THE NARROWEST AND MOST USEFUL ASK: does anything in the tables NOT come from the appraiser roll -- phones, emails, ages, net worth, household members, relatives, prior addresses, financing, any vendor append? None of that is government-record data and the exclusion does not reach it. That is where the exemption definitively stops and it costs them one line to answer. Subject has never lived in or owned property in Florida, so the request itself is a rule-me-out plus prospective-exclusion letter; told them any [PERSONAL] row is somebody else's and to LEAVE IT EXACTLY AS IT IS. ALSO RAISED, AS A SUGGESTION NOT A DEMAND: whether they have a removal process for the people they DO name -- the judge, the police officer, the person with a stalker who happens to own an expensive house. Several states run address confidentiality programmes precisely against published address lists, and a wealth-ranked table with street addresses is what those programmes exist to defeat.
