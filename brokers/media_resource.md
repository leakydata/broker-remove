DIRECT-MAIL LIST COMPANY, and the most substantive letter of the batch, because the opt-out route itself turned out to be the story. Wrote to the ROLE ADDRESS THE POLICY PUBLISHES rather than the named individual on the CA register (149a: the register is a directory, not a doorway) and said so in the letter so they can redirect internally. Opens by asking that a non-designated-method submission be logged as validly submitted. THREE THINGS ABOUT THE ROUTE. (1) THE TELEMARKETING CONSENT CLAUSE, and the reason I did not use the form: the policy states that submitting personal information by and through their website offerings, by clicking the consent button, constitutes an inquiry/application under the Amended Telemarketing Sales Rule and gives prior express written consent under the TCPA -- and that notwithstanding a federal or state DNC listing they retain the right to telemarket, including automated calls and SMS. The opt-out and do-not-sell pages are ALSO forms on that website into which a consumer types a name, address and phone number. I did not assert the clause reaches them -- I cannot tell from outside -- but asked for written confirmation that submitting a do-not-sell or do-not-mail request creates no TSR inquiry and no TCPA consent, and said a consumer should not have to weigh whether asking to be left alone might be recorded as permission to call. (2) THE OPT-OUT LINK LOGS THE VISITOR'S IP: their published client-side JavaScript resolves the visitor's public IP through a third-party lookup and POSTs it with the clicked element and page URL to their own tracking endpoint, and the footer link labelled 'Opt Out' carries exactly that call -- so clicking the link toward the opt-out form creates a fresh record keyed to a network identifier the consumer never supplied, at a company that sells marketing data. Asked whether it is retained, joined, how long, and for its deletion as item (vi). Told them HOW I know, in fairness: the site is served as an un-minified development build, and noted that the same build exposes internal paths and an API token which I have NOT reproduced anywhere -- the token is not in this repo. (3) TWO DIFFERENT RIGHTS UNDER ONE LABEL: the policy says the sale opt-out is a 'Do Not Sell or Share' button; the footer link labelled 'Opt Out' goes to a page whose own text says it adds you to a DO NOT MAIL list. Stopping mail is not opting out of sale, and a person who clicks the link marked Opt Out would reasonably believe otherwise. Asked them to apply both. Then SIX SEPARATELY-NUMBERED REQUESTS, because their policy demands a separate request per right -- know, access, delete, opt-out of sale/sharing/targeted advertising/profiling, do-not-mail, and deletion of the click/IP data -- with an offer to re-send as six emails if intake requires it. WHOSE DATA THE POLICY COVERS: their published policy is almost entirely about website visitors, yet four broker registrations exist because there is a file of people with no relationship to them; asked whether the policy governs that file at all, for its sources, and for the list of 'affiliated sites' it repeatedly references without naming. THE TRUSTEDFORM QUESTION, framed as a question because I checked and did NOT find the script on mrginc.com: the policy says the ActiveProspect TrustedForm Certify SDK is embedded and captures mouse movements, clicks, entered contact data, a page snapshot, IP and headers -- so on which properties does it run, is it on the opt-out pages, and if a certificate exists for a submission of mine it is personal information within this request, held by them and by ActiveProspect. The point: a TrustedForm certificate exists to prove a consumer consented to be contacted, so one generated by a DO-NOT-CONTACT submission is an artifact whose only ordinary use is the opposite of the request that created it. Finally 138: their opt-out flow renders 'All of your personal information has been removed from our systems' IMMEDIATELY on submit, before any search across a marketing file could have run -- asked instead for a confirmation naming which identifiers matched, and said a nil result against named identifiers is a real answer.
