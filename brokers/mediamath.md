## Four bounces, four names, every mailbox full

A single message to `privacy@mediamath.com` produced **four separate non-delivery
reports**, each naming a different individual employee, each saying:

> *"The recipient's mailbox is full and can't accept messages now."*

So the privacy address is not a queue, a ticketing system or a shared mailbox. It is a
**forwarding list of named people** — and every one of them is over quota.

**Two things follow.**

The delivery failure is silent in an unusual way: the exchanger *accepts* the message
before failing to deliver it to any member, so a sender who does not read bounce mail
carefully would record this as sent.

And it says something about the company. Four simultaneously over-quota mailboxes is
what a privacy function looks like **after the staff have gone**. MediaMath's business
has been through insolvency and a change of hands, and this is the residue: an alias
that still resolves, pointed at people who are no longer reading anything.

**So do not keep retrying the address.** The letter already sent asks the right
question for this situation — if a successor, acquirer or affiliate now holds the data,
name the entity and say where to direct the request. A transfer of ownership does not
extinguish a request; it only changes who should answer it. The next move is to
identify the acquirer, not to write to a fuller mailbox.
