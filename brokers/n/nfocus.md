reply opens with a clean statutory refusal:

> *"the state of Pennsylvania does not yet have consumer privacy legislation in
> place... We invite you to reach back out to us once the legislation has been
> officially enacted."*

Taken alone that is the end of it. The next paragraph does the work regardless.
**Do not read the first paragraph and stop.**

**The processor carve-out is stated plainly**, which is better than most:

> *"when NFocus acts as a service provider on behalf of another business, we are
> not able to fulfill consumer rights requests for information processed in that
> role."*

Accurate as far as it goes, and the usual gap applies: it leaves the requester
with a right and no named party to exercise it against.

## The finding: a prior out-of-state address got the request honoured

The paragraph that matters, immediately after the Pennsylvania refusal:

> *"As for your Maryland address: Based on the information you provided, we were
> unable to locate a matching record in our database. However, we have added your
> name and address to our suppression list to help ensure your information is not
> sold in the future."*

**They refused the request as a Pennsylvania matter and granted it as a Maryland
one**, because a prior address in the letter put the request inside a state whose
privacy law they recognise.

Two things follow, and the first is reusable everywhere.

### Prior addresses are not only a search aid

Every letter in this project lists sixteen prior addresses so that records held
against old details get found. That is the stated reason and it is a good one.

**This reply shows a second effect that was never designed for: the list can
change which legal regime a broker applies.** N-Focus looked at an address in
Maryland, decided it was a Maryland request, and acted.

**State the observation carefully.** Coverage under these statutes turns on
residency, not on address history, and a broker choosing to treat a former
address as conferring rights is *their* decision rather than an entitlement to
claim. So this is not a technique to assert — writing "I have rights in Maryland
because I lived there" would be an overreach, and a false one if pressed.

What it is: a reason to **keep listing every prior address in full**, including
out-of-state ones, and never to trim the list for brevity. A broker in a
refusing mood may find a hook in it. Several did not; this one did.

### Suppression without a record, unprompted

They found nothing and suppressed anyway. That is the exact ask this project
makes of every broker — *"if you hold nothing today, please still add a
do-not-add entry"* — and N-Focus volunteered it without being pressed on that
point. Worth quoting at brokers who say a suppression entry is impossible when
no record exists: it is not impossible, it is a choice, and a small agency does
it as a matter of course.

### What was not obtained

The Pennsylvania-scoped part of the request — the full identifier set, hashed
forms, source and date of acquisition, categories of recipient — was declined
along with the state. That refusal is legitimate and was accepted. The
`not_found` status reflects the Maryland answer, which is a real negative plus a
real suppression, not a fob-off.
