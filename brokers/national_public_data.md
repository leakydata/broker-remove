## Gotchas

See the two sections below: the predecessor question, and the removal
route's own limits. The short version:

- **They disclaim the 2024 breach as the former owner's** — which is a claim
  about ownership, not about the data. Ask whether the acquisition included the
  records.
- **The opt-out is keyed to one profile URL.** Do not guess; the wrong URL
  removes a stranger's listing.
- **Their search would not run under automation** — the name field cleared on
  submit while the city field persisted.

## The brand changed hands. That is not the same as the database changing hands.

Their FAQ answers the obvious question before anyone asks it:

> *"Is the current website related to the 2024 data breach?"*
> *"No. We have no affiliation with the data breach that occurred in 2024
> involving the former owner of..."*

Take it at face value — and notice what it does **not** say. A change of *owner*
and a change of *records* are different events, and the sentence only asserts the
first.

> **When a broker disclaims a predecessor, ask whether the acquisition included
> the data.** "We are not them" and "we do not have what they had" are separate
> claims, and only the second one matters to a deletion request. A new owner
> running the same brand over the same records is, from the subject's side,
> indistinguishable from the old one.

Two follow-ups that only they can answer, and both are cheap for them:

- Did the acquisition of the name or domain include the underlying records? A
  plain "no, this was compiled fresh" would close the matter.
- Do they know **who now holds the former owner's database** — successor,
  affiliate, trustee, receiver? That copy is what actually needs reaching, and
  they are far better placed to know than a consumer is.

## The removal route

URL-keyed, and free: search the name plus city and state, open the matching
profile, copy the address-bar URL (`/people/s/<name>/<st>/<city>/<long-id>/`),
paste it at `/optout.html`, press **Request Removal**, then confirm by email.

**Two questions the route itself raises**, both asked and both unanswered so far:

- **Does one URL remove every record, or only that profile?** Their own FAQ
  concedes a common name returns many results. A person with a long address
  history will have several profiles, and if each needs its own submission that
  is `_DEFLECTIONS.md` §33 — the arithmetic deflection — in a new costume.
- **Suppression or one-time removal?** The site describes ongoing compilation
  from public sources, so whether a removed profile is added to a do-not-publish
  list is not hypothetical.

**Do not guess at a profile.** The submission is keyed to a URL, so the wrong URL
removes a stranger's listing and leaves the subject's in place. This one goes to
a human who can recognise their own record.

**What they publish, by their own list**, which is worth knowing before deciding
how hard to push: full name, maiden name, age and date of birth, aliases, marital
status, social media profiles, driving licences; arrest records, convictions,
warrants, court judgments, sex-offender status; property ownership and assessed
values, **eviction information**; relatives, associates and neighbours; cell and
landline numbers with phone-number history; and full address history.
