Standard concession first -- cookie/CTV/bid-stream keyed means a name search returns nothing and that is a real answer -- then the two products that are NOT ordinary audience data. (1) ACR DATA: automatic content recognition identifies what is playing on a television, continuously, so the record is a VIEWING HISTORY -- programmes, films, advertisements, news channels, times, one household. Argued that this is among the most revealing datasets an ad business can hold, because what a person watches indicates political leaning, religious observance, language and national origin, sexual orientation, health concerns and the presence of children NOT as a marketing segment but as a straightforward inference from the content; collected passively from a device bought for another purpose, by a mechanism almost no smart-TV owner could describe. Asked four sub-questions: do they hold ACR tied to any listed identifier or household address, household-vs-device retention and duration, joinability to a name/postal address/hashed email by them or a partner, and enumerable inferred attributes. Deletion AND use-restriction, separately. (2) NEXXEN HEALTH: their own announcement says it is 'powered by PurpleLab', a healthcare data company -- so health information is doing work in the targeting or measurement chain. Asked whether any health segment, condition audience or therapeutic-area attribute attaches to me; what the underlying health data is in category terms and where it comes from; and THE SHARP ONE -- whether matching is on de-identified data and if so AT WHAT POINT an individual is re-identified so an ad can reach them, since if a message can be delivered to a specific person on the basis of a health attribute the pairing exists somewhere even if no single system holds both halves. Exercised the 1798.121 limit-use right. (3) Identity resolution is a named product, so the hashed-email wedge. (4) Their published data partner directory and curated marketplace -- supplier and recipient categories. No IP, no device ID.
