The framing that makes this different from an audience-vendor letter: AN EXCHANGE DOES NOT NEED TO BUILD A PROFILE TO FUNCTION -- it needs to route a bid request and clear an auction -- but every request carries an identifier, a URL, a coarse location and a timestamp, so the question is what happens to them afterwards. Four asks: (1) WHAT SURVIVES THE AUCTION -- are bid requests logged and retained beyond clearing and billing, for how long, and at what granularity (individual rows with identifiers and URLs, or aggregates)? Made the point that a retained bid stream is a browsing history in all but name -- the pages a device visited, in order, with timestamps and approximate location -- which is why the RETENTION answer matters more than the audience answer. (2) Is there a derived layer built from what passes through: segments, interest or intent attributes, identity-graph edges? Those are inferences generated rather than data routed. (3) Where the opt-out lives -- cookie vs server-side -- and whether GPC is recorded durably or honoured only in-session. (4) Supplier and buyer categories. Hashed-email wedge; no device ID, no IP.
