## Gotchas

**Order the asks by what survives.** In a list-rental business the copy held by
the compiler is rarely the only copy, and deleting it alone changes little:

1. **Permanent suppression / do-not-add**, applied when the next compilation
   runs. Without it, the same details are re-acquired from the same upstream
   compiler and re-published.
2. **Source and date of acquisition**, plus — if consent is claimed — the date,
   the form, and the wording shown. A consent nobody can produce is not a consent.
3. **Recipients** over the past two years, with a direction to each to delete.
4. Deletion and opt-out.

**Name the sensitive selects individually and insist on deletion rather than
suppression.** Health condition or medical interest, disability, pregnancy and
presence and age of children, religion, political affiliation and donor history,
ethnicity, income and net worth and credit proxies, financial distress and
bankruptcy indicators, gambling interest, firearms interest, charitable giving.

> A do-not-mail suppression stops the letters arriving and **leaves the attribute
> in the file, still rentable to everyone else.** Those are different outcomes and
> a general deletion request reaches neither, because these attributes sit in a
> separate table and nobody thinks of them as "your personal information".

**Ask about aged, recycled and archived inventory** held apart from the live
database. It is routinely missed by a deletion scoped to current records.
