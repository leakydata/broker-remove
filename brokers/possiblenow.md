- Note: Consent and preference management - so a blanket deletion letter is the WRONG instrument and could harm the subject. A suppression record exists precisely so that the details are not contacted; deleting it removes the protection rather than the exposure. Split the letter in two and said so explicitly: where they hold a SUPPRESSION or PREFERENCE record, do NOT delete it - confirm it exists, say what channels it covers, which clients, how long it persists, and whether anything can be ADDED to strengthen it. Where they hold data for any other purpose - marketing, enrichment, identity resolution, modelling, or anything retained after a client relationship ended - delete that. One extra ask worth reusing: does the suppression record carry MORE than the identifiers needed to suppress? Suppression lists accumulate enrichment the suppression function does not require, and that surplus can be stripped without weakening the protection.
