## Gotchas

**A professional-services firm on a broker list is usually one of four things**,
and they are not equivalent:

- a genuine data-brokerage or data-products line of business;
- a state data-broker registration filed out of caution;
- marketing and business-development data held about non-clients;
- plain mis-categorisation by whoever compiled the list.

A letter premised on the wrong one gets a correct answer to a question nobody
asked, and burns the contact. So the letter says which four it might be, says
that a mis-listing is a perfectly good outcome, and asks in order:

1. **Do you hold anything at all?** With an unqualified negative accepted as
   complete and the matter closed.
2. **In what capacity** — controller, or processor for a named client? A firm of
   this kind holds most personal data on engagements, and "contact the
   controller" is unactionable without the client's name.
3. **Delete anything held for marketing, prospecting or commercial datasets.**
4. **Are you registered as a data broker in any state, and under which entity?**
   Large firms have many legal entities; a registration names one, and that names
   the business the request should reach.

That last question is the useful one. It converts a vague listing into a
checkable fact, and the answer is either a registration number or a denial —
both of which resolve the entry.
