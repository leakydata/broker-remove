## Gotchas

**The two halves of this business need different requests.** A direct-mail
printer holds a mailing file because a client handed it over — that is processor
work, and deleting their copy achieves little while the client still has theirs.
A marketing-services business also builds, appends to and models audiences in its
own right — that is controller work, and deletion bites.

Ask which describes what they hold, and get the clients named if it is the first.

**Then ask for the thing this category is uniquely able to do:** a permanent
**do-not-mail suppression applied across all client campaigns they execute**, not
only across records they own.

> A suppression at the production stage sits where files are processed,
> **regardless of which client supplied them.** For a business that prints and
> mails on behalf of hundreds of clients, that single entry stops more mail than
> deleting their own house file ever would.

If they cannot apply suppression across client work, that answer is worth having
in writing too — it defines the limit of what any request to a production vendor
can achieve.

Name the sensitive selects individually and insist on deletion rather than a
do-not-mail flag: a flag stops the letters and leaves the attribute in the file,
still usable and still licensable.
