## Gotchas

**A tracing product sells the chain, not the endpoint.** Its value is that it
connects a person's former addresses and disconnected numbers to their current
details. So a deletion scoped to the current record leaves exactly the thing
being sold:

> Ask for the **linkage** between former addresses, former telephone numbers and
> current details to be deleted, not only the current row. Deleting the endpoint
> while keeping the chain leaves the person findable by walking it.

**Relatives and associates are the product here, not a side-effect.** On a
people-search site the related-person listing is a secondary route; on a tracing
product it is the primary one, because a tracer who cannot find the subject
directly follows the connections. Frame it that way in the letter.

**Ask whether enquiry logs are retained.** If someone has run a trace on the
subject, a record of that search may exist. Ask whether such logs are kept and
for how long — not *who* searched, which they will not answer and which is not
the point. Whether the logs exist at all is the useful fact.
