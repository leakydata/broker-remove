### The Cc is the family evidence

This is a cleaner family signal than most, and it costs nothing to spot.

> **When a reply arrives from one brand's privacy address with another brand's
> privacy address on Cc, the second brand does not run its own privacy desk.**
> The Cc is not a courtesy — it is the shared team keeping the acquired brand's
> mailbox in the loop on a request it did not handle.

So the group answering here is **Stirista + Lighthouse List + Media Source
Solutions + Andrews Wharton** — four entries in the tracker, one team, one
template, one search. Adds to [[_BROKER_FAMILIES]] as a variant of signal 3
(a privacy address on another brand's domain), except the direction is reversed:
here the *reply* carries the other brand rather than the published policy.

Practical consequence: these four should be written to as one family in future,
but the letters were worth sending separately this time — each produced its own
ticket number, and a ticket per brand is better evidence than one ticket claimed
to cover four.

### Why "not found" is the good outcome here

Most null results are a dead end: the company says it holds nothing, and next
quarter a fresh feed puts you back in. This one is different, and the difference
is the second clause.

> **"Not found" plus "added to our opt-out list for the future" is a
> *prospective* answer.** It converts an absence today into a standing rule.
> That is the outcome the suppression question is normally asked to extract —
> volunteered here without being asked twice.

Record it as `not_found`, not as an unanswered request. And note what was *not*
required to get it: no account, no ID document, no profile URL, no per-record
submission cap.
