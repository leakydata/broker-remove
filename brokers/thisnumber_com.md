THE FINDING (SILENT_FAILURES 262): their privacy policy -- last revised 29 Sep 2018, pre-CCPA -- covers cookies, Google Analytics, DoubleClick, Cloudflare, IP anonymisation and comment metadata, and contains ZERO mention of the phone listings: not the sources, not retention, not sharing, not how a person named in one gets it removed. The policy governs the VISITOR DOING THE LOOKUP and says nothing about the PERSON BEING LOOKED UP. Practical bite: my standard fallback ask -- 'if you are not subject to these statutes, honour this under your published policy' -- has nothing to attach to. THE LEVER I USED INSTEAD: they declare in terms 'ThisNumber.com is the Data Controller of your personal data provided to, or collected by or for, or processed in connection with, our Service.' Data collected FOR and processed IN CONNECTION WITH the Service plainly includes the directory -- the listings are not incidental to the Service, they ARE it. So they have declared themselves controller of the listing data by their own words, and the controller duties their own GDPR-shaped policy recites (erasure, rectification, objection, access INCLUDING SOURCE) attach without me having to establish which statute binds an international site. Asked for: name-to-number association removed but NOT the numbers delisted (11 prior numbers are reassigned -- suppress the link, not the value, per 193); source of each listing; suppression against re-import; no account, no ID. Also told them there is no removal route anywhere on the site, so the requests they get are only from people persistent enough to hunt for an address.
