- Note: Directory-sourced, and the clearest case yet for SF 235's entity-name question. Thryv is the successor to the DEX MEDIA / YP (Yellow Pages) businesses, so if a registration exists it plausibly sits under a predecessor name and a search by trading name would never find it. Asked directly, with the reason given. TWO PIECES OF EVIDENCE THE LISTING IS RIGHT, shown rather than asserted: their own footer publishes a 'Directory Opt-Out' alongside 'Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information' -- a company offering a directory opt-out has a directory to opt out of -- and the Dex/YP lineage. THE SUBSTANCE, which is a category this project has not written to before: PRINT DIRECTORY DATA. A Yellow Pages listing was for most of its history something a person was in BY DEFAULT -- you got a telephone line and you were listed unless you paid to be ex-directory. Nobody consented in any sense we would now recognise, and the listings outlived the phone lines. An address and number printed in 1998 is still an address and number, and it is exactly the stale, address-keyed record a people-search compiler buys. So the letter leads with the SIXTEEN FORMER ADDRESSES and ten former numbers as the likely keys and says the current details are the least likely to match. ONWARD-RECIPIENTS ASK SHARPENED: a directory licensed onward for decades is the clearest case I have of a deletion that cannot travel, so asked them to say plainly if they cannot reach the licensees rather than let me assume. 193 LIMIT AT ITS MOST CONCRETE: in a DIRECTORY product, excluding a bare address or number removes THAT HOUSEHOLD'S LISTING -- a stranger suppressed on my say-so. Told them that if the system can only exclude bare values, leave the former ones alone: I would rather stay listed at fifteen old addresses than have fifteen households removed on my account.
