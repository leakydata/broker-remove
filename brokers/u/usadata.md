THE APPARENT CONTRADICTION, DEFUSED RATHER THAN EXPLOITED: my letter quoted their site's '260M+ consumer records'; their reply says they 'do not maintain [their] own proprietary consumer database'. Told them I do not think those are inconsistent -- they reconcile if the 260M is LICENSED rather than owned, which is what their own section 2 describes. The marketing sentence is about what they can reach; the privacy sentence about what they originate. BUT THE RECONCILIATION IS WHAT PRODUCES THE QUESTION: their own phrase is 'licensed/provider-supplied data AVAILABLE IN OUR ENVIRONMENT', so A COPY EXISTS AT USADATA, not merely a pointer to Acxiom's file. Two consequences: a suppression at Acxiom fixes the NEXT licence load, not the current copy -- which is why their own suppression matters; and it does not reach anything already ACTIVATED to a CTV/social/email platform for a client campaign, which outlives deletion both here and at source. AND ONE HONEST DOUBT, framed carefully as not-calling-them-liars: a 260-million-record consumer file is near enough every US adult, and a true nil for a 47-year-old with sixteen addresses and eleven phone numbers WOULD BE REMARKABLE -- not impossible, but worth confirming. So the narrow question: was the match run against EVERY identifier, or against the current name and address? My original letter had asked whether their matching handles more than one name-and-address combination per request and offered to submit separately if not; that went unanswered and it is the question that decides how to read the nil. Said plainly I would send sixteen requests without complaint if that is the system limit -- a reasonable limit, and discovering it months later is the bad outcome. Also asked whether HASHED forms were searched, since an address travels to an activation platform as a digest and a plaintext-only search misses by construction rather than by absence. Two on the suppression: what it is keyed to (all identifiers or only the sending address), and whether it survives a NEW LICENCE LOAD from the three named providers -- the event it exists for. A suppression checked at query time but not at ingest leaves the record present and merely unreturned, which is weaker. SF 193 association limit repeated.
