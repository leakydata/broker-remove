- Note: NEW ROW, never previously contacted, surfaced by the SF 242 opt-out-denial query. Wiland's 2025 filing: 790 opt-out requests received, 312 complied in whole, 478 DENIED, with the notes field saying 'Denials of deletion and opt-out requests were due to the failure of the requester to sufficiently authenticate the requester's or the individual's identity.' THE ARGUMENT, PUT AS A QUESTION NOT A COMPLAINT: an opt-out of sale or sharing is NOT a verifiable consumer request -- the regulations bar requiring identity verification as a condition of honouring one, because requiring proof of identity to stop a sale would defeat the right and there is nothing to protect against. The consumer is not asking to receive data, alter a record or have anything disclosed; THEY ARE ASKING TO BE EXCLUDED. If identifiers match nothing the exclusion costs nothing and applies prospectively; if they match something the exclusion IS the right. Conceded the two legitimate grounds explicitly -- good-faith documented belief of fraud, and proof of authorisation from an AGENT -- and noted those are different reasons from the one their note gives. THE SHARPEST POINT COMES FROM THEIR OWN FILING: 'We don't possess or utilize sensitive data like credit card or bank account numbers, Social Security Numbers or other government-issued identifiers.' So what they hold is name, address, email and modelled attributes -- EXACTLY WHAT A REQUESTER SUPPLIES -- and there is nothing else to authenticate against. A supplied detail that matches no record is A NIL RESULT, NOT AN AUTHENTICATION FAILURE, and the two send opposite messages: 'you were never here' versus 'we did not believe you'. Asked what the check compares against and what would satisfy it; whether any of the 478 were in fact no-matching-record, in which case 478 people believe they were refused when they were never in the file; and -- the outcome I actually want -- whether they will apply the opt-out PROSPECTIVELY regardless, since a standing exclusion against identifiers they may not hold today is the only thing that survives their next acquisition. DELIBERATELY GAVE THEM CREDIT, because it is true: their practices note is the longest and most specific on the register -- products may not be used for credit, employment or housing decisions; no health information accepted or generated; no knowing collection about children; executive approval required for new client engagements. Most registrants write 'N/A'. Said that is why I wrote a question rather than a complaint: a company that takes the trouble to write all that is not hiding behind a verification screen, so the 478 probably has a mundane explanation and I would rather hear it than assume one. Also asked for deletion of MODELLED attributes specifically -- propensity and value scores, donor/buyer likelihood, wealth estimates, life-event flags -- since those are the product and a row stripped of its name but keeping them against a household or hashed key has been RENAMED rather than deleted.
